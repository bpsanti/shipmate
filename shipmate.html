What the picture shows
  • Each ship is a project with live work. The flagship on the left is firstmate's, and firstmate is at its helm.
  • Each hand is one agent, and where it stands is its phase: in the crow's nest with a spyglass, exploring; on deck hauling rope or hammering, implementing; at the bow with signal flags, PR open; fishing at the rail, waiting for CI; on the gangplank with a sea bag, ready to merge. A question mark means the phase is unknown.
  • A red flag means the hand needs the captain: its crew is parked, blocked or failed, or a needs-decision or blocked line is still open. Its name stays shown.
  • A scout rows a skiff out to the horizon, rows back holding the map (the report is being written), and moors at the pier when the report is delivered.
  • The barrels in the flagship's hold are the Claude accounts' quota: the amber level is what is left, and a red barrel runs out before it resets or has less than 15% left.
  • The flagship's draught is firstmate's context: the fuller it is, the lower she sits, and past the warning threshold her white load line goes under water.
  • The boats moored under the flagship are the queue; a red flag marks one held for the captain's decision. Crates on the pier are tasks delivered in the last 7 days.
  • The sea is calm, choppy or rough with the machine's load. Hover a hand, boat, barrel or crate for its name; click a hand or a boat for the same details as its card.
